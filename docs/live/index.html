Run ATLAS live, in your browser

This page runs ATLAS's real code on your own device: a simulated ESP32 signs your payment, and ATLAS checks the device's signature and replay counter, applies the customer's owner-signed policy and its ML evidence, signs its decision, and the bank verifies that signature. Nothing is sent to any server — there is no ATLAS server.

Before you start

  • It downloads about 39 MB once — Python for the browser (Pyodide 314.0.7) and its libraries — from cdn.jsdelivr.net. The loader is integrity-checked, and Pyodide checks every package against its own hash list.
  • The first start takes 20 seconds to a little over a minute, depending on your browser and connection (Chrome-based browsers are quickest, Safari slower); later visits are faster, from your browser's cache. Wi-Fi recommended on mobile.
  • Keys, stores and the ML model are made fresh for you, in this tab's memory, and vanish when you close it.

    The customer's policy

      If several rules match, the strictest action wins (DENY over STEP-UP over ALLOW). Payee novelty is recomputed from the customer's own payments — the device cannot claim it.

      What is simulated, plainly. The ESP32 is the firmware's tested Python twin (firmware/virtual_device.py), and ATLAS and the bank run in this tab instead of across a network. Three browser adapters make that possible without changing ATLAS's code — FastAPI's thread pool becomes a direct call (browsers have no threads); ATLAS's request to the bank is handed to the bank's real verify_endpoint(); and key protection's scrypt comes from cryptography (byte-identical). A parity test proves they change no decision. Payments are dated yesterday so the time rules are stable.
