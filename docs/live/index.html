Live simulation · real ATLAS code

Run ATLAS live, in your browser

This page runs ATLAS's real code on your own device: a simulated ESP32 signs your payment, and ATLAS checks the device's signature and replay counter, applies the customer's owner-signed policy and its ML evidence, signs its decision, and the bank verifies that signature. Nothing is sent to any server: there is no ATLAS server.

  • Runs entirely in this tab
  • Code checked against its published SHA-256
  • Fresh keys for every visitor

Before you start

  • It downloads about 39 MB once: Python for the browser (Pyodide 314.0.7) and its libraries, from cdn.jsdelivr.net. The loader is integrity-checked, and Pyodide checks every package against its own hash list.
  • The first start takes 20 seconds to a little over a minute, depending on your browser and connection (Chrome-based browsers are quickest, Safari slower); later visits are faster, from your browser's cache. Wi-Fi recommended on mobile.
  • Keys, stores and the ML model are made fresh for you, in this tab's memory, and vanish when you close it.

    Your real location · optional

    If you allow it, your browser tells this page where you are, and every payment carries that position as signed evidence. It goes only to ATLAS running in this tab: it is never sent anywhere, never shown on the page and gone when you close the tab. ATLAS answers with a grade only, inside or outside your home area, and a position from a browser is always graded LOW confidence: the browser does not say whether satellites, Wi-Fi or the network placed you.

      Start-up log

      What is simulated, plainly. The ESP32 is the firmware's tested Python twin (firmware/virtual_device.py), and ATLAS and the bank run in this tab instead of across a network. Three browser adapters make that possible without changing ATLAS's code: FastAPI's thread pool becomes a direct call (browsers have no threads); ATLAS's request to the bank is handed to the bank's real verify_endpoint(); and key protection's scrypt comes from cryptography (byte-identical). A parity test proves they change no decision. Payments are dated yesterday so the time rules are stable. The trace and the animations only draw what ATLAS answered; they never decide anything.

      Location, plainly. If you share it, your location is real: your own browser's position, used only in this tab. Location is evidence, never proof (a browser reports whatever the device tells it), so it can only add a check: being "at home" relaxes no rule. The ESP32 in the Wokwi simulator has a GNSS receiver too, and that one is simulated, with scripted positions.
